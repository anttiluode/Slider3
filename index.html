03 / physical and mathematical provenance

Where we follow the paper—and where we don't

The OpenAI manuscript constructs a positive, conserved lifted wave-current tensor in additional coordinates, then uses a planar auxiliary Dirichlet potential to obtain bounds. Slider3 borrows the coherent summation, a polarization disk, and a positive screened inverse. Our finite-difference surrogate uses an isotropic spatial Laplacian, not the paper's directional, variable-coefficient geometry. Its channels are approximate, and no conservation theorem is claimed.

The three fields are synthetic periodic 2D spatial waves—not quantum dynamics or microphone recordings. Slider2's actual cable-and-speaker loop remains an earlier, distinct hardware experiment.

What would count as a useful new result?

Not merely prettier interference images. The auxiliary potential would have to improve a predeclared task—say predicting which source produced an unknown noisy field, or tracking stable coherent relationships—over raw signal energy, standard phase coherence, a coherent density without the potential, and simpler spatial smoothing, at comparable observation and computation costs. This first page tests mathematical feasibility and exposes those controls; it does not yet demonstrate a neural learning rule.
